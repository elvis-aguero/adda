\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

This section is a \emph{register}, not a narrative. One entry per mechanism,
each owing the reader the same three things: what it does, what it is instead
of, and what it costs. The shape is enforced by a macro, so an entry that
omits its cost is a visible hole rather than a stylistic choice.

It is written to grow past any page limit on purpose and is cut at submission.
A design decision taken and not recorded here is lost; a decision recorded and
later cut costs nothing.

\textbf{Write each entry as non-technically as its subject allows} --- see rule
0 in \texttt{paper/README.md}. Say what a mechanism DOES rather than what it is
called; name an identifier only where the name is itself the decision or a
reader would need it to find the thing. This section is where the rule slips
first, because every entry is written just after the code it describes and
arrives carrying that code's vocabulary. The reader is a researcher deciding
whether to believe the design, not somebody who will maintain it.

\subsection{The graph and the open loop}

\decision{Hub-and-spoke topology}
{A strategizer holds outgoing edges to a literature reviewer, a data
generator, an implementer and a critic. Behaviour follows topology rather than
role: any node with an outgoing edge orchestrates, whatever it is called, so a
two- or three-node study runs without a separate code path.}
{a fixed pipeline of stages, or a fully connected team. The hub keeps one agent
accountable for the run's scientific direction while letting specialists be
swapped or removed.}
{the hub is a bottleneck and carries the longest context. Removing a specialist
is a graph edit --- which is what makes the evaluation's leave-one-out arms
cheap.}

\decision{Delegation is asynchronous, and harvesting is one call}
{Work is dispatched without blocking, and a bare \texttt{Wait()} returns
whichever delegation finishes first, so $N$ in flight are drained by $N$ calls.
It refuses rather than hanging when every open delegation is parked on a
question or has died without reporting.}
{a single-target wait, which left polling as the only way to collect a fan-out
--- and polling is discouraged elsewhere in the prompt, so the two rules
quietly opposed each other.}
{concurrency is a behaviour, not a guarantee. Across 39 cluster runs, reliance
on the single-target wait predicted serial execution ($r=-0.54$ against mean
concurrent delegations, controlling for duration) at a measured mean
concurrency of 1.21 on a median of 15 delegations per run.}

\subsection{The epistemic layer}

Four mechanisms, deliberately separable so each can be ablated alone.

\decision{Hypothesis ledger}
{An append-only record of falsifiable hypotheses and their verdicts (OPEN,
SUPPORTED, FALSIFIED, INCONCLUSIVE), mutated only through tools and never
edited directly. Every delegation cites the hypothesis it tests.}
{letting the agent keep its claims in its context window. The ledger survives
context compaction, is auditable afterwards, and makes ``what did this run
actually claim'' a file rather than a reading exercise.}
{prompt surface and tool calls; and because delegations must cite a hypothesis,
it shapes how work is decomposed --- a benefit or a constraint depending on the
problem.}

The record is strict, and the strictness is the mechanism: a claim with no
falsification criterion cannot be written down at all.

\begin{verbatim}
{"id": "H1",
 "statement": "Optimal t/L is near 0.08 - thin walls maximise buckling",
 "falsification_criterion": "a point with t/L in [0.10,0.14] beats
                             buckling_load_norm 1.47",
 "prediction": "best at t/L ~ 0.08 +/- 0.02",
 "prior": 0.55, "posterior": 0.78,
 "status_log": [{"status": "OPEN",      "delegation": null},
                {"status": "SUPPORTED", "delegation": "D006",
                 "numbers": {"buckling_load_norm": 1.62}}]}
\end{verbatim}

\decision{Falsification charter}
{One binding text defining how a hypothesis may be tested and labelled ---
severity of the attempt, verdict follows the result, no goalpost-moving ---
spliced into both the strategizer's and the critic's prompts so both cite the
same standard.}
{per-agent instructions that drift apart. A shared constant is the cheapest way
to make two agents answerable to one rule.}
{a fixed block of prompt in two roles, and a rule the agent can be held to only
as well as the referee below enforces it.}

\decision{Live verdict referee}
{When a hypothesis is closed, an independent judge checks \emph{at the moment
of assertion} that the verdict obeys the charter, and is fed its own prior
rulings on the same hypothesis so a borderline verdict cannot silently flip. It
runs on the critic's model, not the strategizer's, and is advisory: on timeout
or failure the verdict stands.}
{checking only at the end, when the run has already been built on the claim.}
{an extra model call per verdict, budgeted tight --- a hung call once froze a
run for roughly 89 minutes, which is why the timeout exists. Activation
\measured{8.7\% of runs}{studies/run\_ledger.csv}, which makes it descriptive
rather than inferential in any ablation.}

\decision{Drift monitor}
{Background rules bracketing the evaluation ledger from both directions ---
evaluations reported but never written, and rows written with no attributable
owner --- escalating repeated drift to the critic.}
{trusting the agent's account of what it evaluated.}
{activation \measured{19.6\%}{studies/run\_ledger.csv}; also descriptive rather
than inferential.}

\decision{Process milestones}
{A small backlog --- assess the literature, get the oracle ready --- that gates
delegation to the implementer until the strategizer resolves each one, by
completing it or skipping it with a reason.}
{trusting that an agent will orient itself before spending real evaluations. The
gate is on the ONE node that consumes budget, so exploration stays free.}
{it fires in \measured{93.5\% of runs}{studies/run\_ledger.csv}, so it is the
highest-dose mechanism here and the one most likely to be shaping behaviour in
ways not attributed to it. Skipping is soft, which means a determined agent can
clear the backlog without doing the work.}

\decision{Pre-registration reaches the experimenter}
{The registered prediction and its falsification criterion are injected into the
worker's brief, so the agent running the experiment can read the claim it is
testing.}
{registering a prediction where only the strategizer can see it. A criterion the
experimenter never reads cannot discipline the experiment; it can only be
checked against afterwards, when the work is already done.}
{it couples two prompts, and it lets a worker optimise toward the criterion
rather than toward the truth --- which is the same hazard pre-registration
carries in human science, and is why the referee above judges the verdict
rather than the result.}

\subsection{The deliverable and the reproduction gate}

\decision{Reproduce before close}
{The run closes on a notebook that has been executed and reproduced
\emph{before} the close is allowed. A deliverable that fails to reproduce after
a bounded number of sighted attempts closes the run FAILED --- loudly, and
distinctly from a conclusion that was merely never validated.}
{a report plus a promise. This converts ``the agent says it found $X$'' into
``here is a notebook that re-derives $X$ from the recorded data'', and is the
mechanism most likely to be the headline for a mechanics audience.}
{it constrains what the agent may produce, and the gate is a real failure mode:
reproduction bounces in \measured{13.0\% of runs}{studies/run\_ledger.csv}.}

\subsection{Ground truth and the ledger}
\label{sec:oracle}

\decision{One metered door to the oracle}
{Every ground-truth evaluation goes through \texttt{get\_evaluator()}, which
wraps the registered generator so that each call is written to the canonical
\texttt{ExperimentData} store with its provenance, under a file lock. The
wrapper is not exported: an agent cannot construct a store-redirected
evaluator.}
{letting an agent call the objective function and report what it found. The
ledger is then the run's evidence, and a claim is checkable against rows rather
than against the agent's account of them --- which is what makes the
reproduction gate above possible at all.}
{one door is a bottleneck by design, and everything cheap that is NOT an
evaluation (surrogate predictions, analysis) has to be explicitly off-ledger or
the meter stops meaning anything.}

\decision{Design namespaces: several oracles in one run}
{A run may carry more than one oracle, one per design parametrization the agent
invents, each with its own isolated store. \texttt{Delegate(..., namespace=…)}
scopes a worker to one, injected through the environment so the agent's call
site stays a bare \texttt{get\_evaluator()}.}
{one fixed design space per run. Open design-space discovery means the agent
proposes a parametrization the study did not anticipate, and it needs somewhere
to evaluate it that cannot contaminate the baseline.}
{additive by construction --- \texttt{namespace=None} is byte-for-byte the
single-study path --- but it means ``the ledger'' is $N$ ledgers, and the
single-store load idiom silently reads only the default. That is a live trap,
which is why the deliverable spec names the multi-store call.}

\decision{Counts are derived from the ledger, not remembered}
{A report-time tool reads per-experiment and per-delegation evaluation counts
live from the stores, and prints budget spent against remaining.}
{letting the agent quote a number from its plan. Both failure modes are
observed, not hypothetical: one run hardcoded 70 evaluations where the ledger
held 90, and another asserted 200 remaining with 200 spent. Both closed
UNGATED.}
{a discipline the prompt has to insist on --- the agent must call it rather
than recall. It is folded into the tool the agent already uses to summarise the
recorded data, rather than being one more tool to know about.}

\subsection{Anti-drift: generated, not typed}
\label{sec:antidrift}

\decision{The prompt is derived from the live system}
{The tool catalogue is rendered from the live closure dictionary at every
invocation; the run diagram is generated from the live graph; a feature owns
its knob, its tools and its prompt section as one unit; and the documented
run-knob table is checked against the source, so a knob read but undocumented,
documented but unread, or documented with the wrong default fails the build.
Every tool shows the agent at least one example call, and each example is
checked against the tool it belongs to, so an example cannot teach a call that
would fail.}
{documentation and prompts maintained in parallel with the code. The failure
this prevents is specific and was observed repeatedly: a flag that appears to
disable a feature while the agent is still instructed to use it.}
{generation makes the assembled prompt harder to read as one artifact, which is
why a provenance map exists citing every prompt fragment back to its source
line. And the guarantee is only as good as its contract tests: one such test
was silently reduced to checking one of seven feature defaults by a refactor
that moved defaults into the feature registry, and reported as passing skips
throughout.}

\decision{Few tools, each owning one object}
{Operations on the same thing are one tool. The notebook is created, edited and
pruned through a single tool; a hypothesis is listed or read in full through
one; a process milestone is added, completed or skipped through one. The
strategizer's thirteen notebook, hypothesis, milestone and status tools became
three.}
{a tool per operation. Every tool is a choice the agent makes on every call,
and tools that differ only in which arguments they take invite the wrong
choice --- the agent had already been sending one tool the arguments meant
for its sibling, which the tool quietly ignored.}
{a tool whose behaviour depends on the arguments it is given, which its
description and its examples have to make plain.}

\subsection{Provenance: recorded, not reconstructed}

\decision{Terminal state is a value, not a grep}
{The run records rather than re-derives its outcome, \emph{how} it terminated,
\emph{whether anything reviewed it}, the resolved configuration it actually ran
with after precedence, and per-call telemetry by role, phase and model.}
{inferring these afterwards from artifacts.}
{a schema to maintain, and a migration for historical rows which cannot be
back-filled honestly and are left blank. The argument for paying it is
empirical and is the strongest single piece of evidence here for the whole
provenance stance: when the outcome \emph{was} inferred --- grepped from the
run's own final report --- backstop kills and critic-less runs both recorded as
validated successes, and those rows are still in the ledger.}

\decision{One git repository per run workspace}
{The run's delegation workspace is a git repository of its own, with one commit
per delegation --- succeeded and failed alike --- and the resulting sha stamped
onto that delegation's record.}
{taking a delegation's word for which files it changed. The reproduction gate
proves the notebook runs; it cannot prove a worker's account of its own edits is
faithful.}
{the agents get no git tool and never see the repository: the harness commits on
their behalf, because an agent that can rewrite the history recording its work
defeats the purpose. Never fatal --- no git, or a failed commit, records a null
sha and the run proceeds.}

\subsection{Watching and steering a run in flight}

\decision{A read-only live viewer}
{A local web UI serves the agent graph with a live status per node and, on
click, that node's running transcript. Read-only, and a separate process from
the run.}
{reading a log after the fact. A multi-hour autonomous run that has gone wrong
in the first ten minutes is the expensive failure, and nothing in a transcript
tells you that while it is still cheap to stop.}
{it is a second consumer of the run's on-disk state, so anything it displays is
a format the run is now committed to writing.}

\decision{The operator channel is the file system}
{A human can answer a worker's question, queue a note for the entry node, or
address a note at a specific running delegation --- all as small JSON files in
the run's own debug directory.}
{a socket or an RPC. The run and the viewer are separate processes, so the file
system is the simplest channel that works --- and it makes the whole exchange
part of the run record rather than terminal scrollback.}
{it introduces a watch heartbeat, because a run must be able to tell
waiting-for-an-answer apart from stalling on a question nobody can see.}

\subsection{Resource governance}

\decision{One hard boundary, soft budgets everywhere else}
{A memory cap for host safety is hard. Everything else is soft: the evaluation
budget nudges rather than stops, and wall-clock and cost act as resumable
backstops rather than crashes.}
{hard limits on the science. A budget that kills a run mid-conclusion destroys
the run's value, so backstops checkpoint and halt resumably.}
{a resumable halt is a state the analysis must handle, and it is the source of
the censoring problem in \cref{sec:evaluation}.}

\decision{A wall-clock watchdog outside the run}
{A hard timer at twice the run's time budget force-exits a stalled run and
recursively kills every process tree it spawned, including detached ones a
process-group kill would miss. It leaves a labelled post-mortem.}
{trusting the run's own backstops. Those are inside the process that may be the
thing that is stuck.}
{it is an EXTERNAL cutoff, not an error condition, and it is the source of the
censoring problem in \cref{sec:evaluation}: killed runs carry no token or cost
data, and at least one had already reached a passing critic verdict. It is
never pooled with failure.}

\decision{Compaction rather than trimming, when the window fills}
{When a conversation outgrows the served context window, the middle is replaced
by a generated summary that keeps numerical results and the delegation each came
from, decisions and their reasons, and what was tried and failed. The first user
turn is never evicted.}
{dropping the oldest messages, which is free and deterministic and also blind:
the message it evicts may be the delegation report the whole run turns on, and
nothing downstream can tell that it went.}
{a summary is model output, so it can be wrong in ways a dropped message cannot
--- it can quietly restate a number. So it is fenced and labelled in the
transcript, the full transcript still lands on disk, and the run records which
policy it used so an analysis can condition on it. A failure to summarise falls
back to trimming rather than failing the turn.}

\subsection{Knowledge the agent cannot hold in context}
\label{sec:knowledge}

Five bodies of reference material are indexed rather than injected. The design
of each is dictated by the form the material takes, and the differences between
them are the interesting part.

\decision{f3dasm: runtime introspection}
{The installed f3dasm is imported and introspected at run time --- 243 symbols,
roughly 117{,}000 characters of docstring --- and exposed as a single lookup
tool to the two agents that write f3dasm code, and to the one that decides
what they write, which otherwise guesses what f3dasm provides. Each entry leads with the
\emph{canonical public import path}, resolved by walking the public
namespaces.}
{parsing a source checkout. On a cluster the checkout may be a different
version or absent, so an index built from the live import describes the exact
code the run will execute against and cannot go stale. The public-path
resolution is not a refinement but the point: \texttt{\_\_module\_\_} reports
the private definition site for every public f3dasm symbol, and an index that
handed that to an agent would produce fragile private imports --- worse than no
tool.}
{importing the package, with its side effects. Only 47 of 125 defined symbols
are publicly reachable; the other 78 are answerable but marked ``do not import
this'', so a lookup cannot be mistaken for an endorsement.}

\decision{Abaqus: offline extraction, BM25 over structure}
{Licensed documentation HTML is parsed once into a page database and searched
with BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25} over weighted title, abstract and body
fields. The builder ships; the corpus does not.}
{flattening pages to text. A survey of 1{,}200 pages found 99\% carry tables,
52\% MathML and 37\% preformatted blocks, with parameter documentation in
definition lists --- exactly the content a reference lookup needs, and exactly
what flattening destroys. BM25 rather than the f3dasm ranker because this
corpus is large and wildly non-uniform: document-length normalisation and
inverse document frequency both matter here and neither is available from a
few hundred uniform symbol entries.}
{the corpus cannot be redistributed, so the feature is opt-in and unmeasured
until a user supplies one. Exact-token search also needs a spelling bridge,
since every Abaqus keyword begins with a character the tokeniser discards.}

\decision{adda: the same introspection, a different unit mix}
{adda indexes itself for \emph{other people's} agents, exposed as
\texttt{adda-docs}. Four unit kinds are indexed: symbols, module docstrings,
declared module-level constants (each carrying its module's docstring), and the
knowledge-base markdown shipped inside the package.}
{indexing symbols alone, which is what f3dasm needs. Measured on 128 labelled
queries, symbols alone \emph{lose} to \texttt{ripgrep}; all four together beat
it. The same four units measured on f3dasm make it worse. The unit mix is a
property of where a package keeps its knowledge, not a global setting: 23\% of
adda's docstring mass is in module docstrings, and its rules live in markdown.}
{a fourth index to maintain, and a known weakness --- there is no minimum-score
floor, so the tool almost never reports that it does not know, and a
confidently wrong answer costs an agent more than a miss.}

\decision{Basilisk: the source tree, at the granularity it is authored in}
{The Basilisk CFD source is indexed as solver headers and worked cases, with
each header's entry naming what it is VERIFIED WITH and what it is NEVER
STACKED WITH --- derived from what 213 compiling cases actually include
together.}
{an abstract-syntax index. Measured on the real tree: a C grammar encodes
operator precedence as depth, so 59k lines becomes roughly two million nodes at
depth 100 with three-quarters carrying no information; Basilisk C is a DSL, so
a C parser emits error nodes on exactly the constructs carrying the physics and
discards as comments the literate prose that is 16\% of every solver file; and
the headers do not parse standalone even with Basilisk's own parser.}
{the compatibility signal is an ABSENCE, and absences lie. Raw co-occurrence
called two headers incompatible when one reaches the other transitively, which
would invent a constraint the physics does not have --- worse than a miss. The
fix excludes each header's transitive closure and ignores any header too thinly
used for its absence to mean anything.}

\decision{Literature: a study-scoped corpus of primary sources}
{Papers the literature reviewer acquires are chunked, embedded and searched, and
the corpus persists across runs of a study.}
{re-acquiring and re-embedding per run, which is waste.}
{it is a contamination channel: a run can answer using papers an earlier run
fetched, so any arm that ablates the literature stack needs a cold corpus or an
explicit note. Recorded in \cref{sec:threats} as accepted rather than fixed.}

\decision{One contract across all five, and deliberately not one mechanism}
{Every provider answers \texttt{consult(query, limit)}: a key returns its
entry, a phrase returns a menu, a miss says the miss is about THIS index rather
than about reality, the reply is capped, and an unconfigured provider returns no
tool at all rather than one that errors.}
{five providers converging by imitation, which is what had happened. Imitation
does not fail when somebody drifts, and two providers had shipped with no reply
cap at all --- defeating the one thing the tools exist for.}
{the MECHANISMS stay different, on evidence: f3dasm gets worse with adda's unit
mix ($\mathrm{r@}1$ $0.77 \to 0.62$) and adda loses to \texttt{ripgrep}
without it, while Abaqus needs length normalisation that f3dasm's uniform
entries neither have nor need. Forcing one ranker would make three providers
worse to make a table look tidy.}

\subsection{What this section still owes}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The alternatives in the author's voice.} Most ``instead of''
    lines are reconstructed from code comments. The real abandoned
    alternatives are in commit messages and the backlog, and are more
    convincing than a retrospective rationalisation.
  \item \textbf{A worked example.} One run end to end with its actual
    artifacts: problem statement in, hypotheses proposed, a delegation, a
    verdict and its referee ruling, the gate firing, the notebook out.
  \item \textbf{Quantified costs.} Several costs asserted qualitatively above
    are measurable from telemetry and should be numbers.
\end{enumerate}
